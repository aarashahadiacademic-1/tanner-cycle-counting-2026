# Tanner-graph cycle counting (2026)

Fresh experimental measurements, not copied from manuscript tables. Exactly 10 same-target exact-versus-KB comparisons and 11 girth-count comparisons (KB, exact, one-processor hybrid).

- `table1_rebuilt.csv`: exact and KB times (median of seven measurements).
- `table2_rebuilt.csv`: KB/exact medians of seven, hybrid mean and empirical MARE over 100 randomized runs.
- `graphs/`: fixed edge lists for A/B/C/D/R/P benchmark instances. Q410 and Q412 are deterministically generated by `generate_qc.py` (their SHA-256 digests are in table II).
- `generate.py`, `generate_diverse.py`, `generate_qc.py`: construction sources for the benchmark families.

Parameters: C++17 `g++ -O3`, epsilon=0.08, r=101, constant Q=4096 *declared work credits* for interleaving; root selection and each sampled walk step are charged 1 and 8 credits per step, respectively, while the exact search charges BFS/DFS visits, adjacency inspections and marking updates. The exact routine is resumed *between entire roots* to avoid extreme per-operation state-machine overhead. A root may therefore overshoot the quantum: Q412's observed maximum root cost was 4527 credits. The correct accounting includes a maximum-root overshoot term, not an unconditional 2Q additive term. The schedule is not identical to the strict atomic-operation scheduler of the theoretical manuscript.

The experiment compares actual wall-clock times, which are dependent on hardware and scheduling. No guaranteed wall-clock factor versus an independently optimized implementation is asserted. The KB implementation is our own, and its results agree with exact counts on all listed targets. Input generation and correctness verification are untimed. Fixed synthetic Tanner graphs are not standardized LDPC codes. Existing historical repositories were not used as sources of manuscript table timings.
